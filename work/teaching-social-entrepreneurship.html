Teaching

Social Entrepreneurship, National College

I teach social entrepreneurship to second-year undergraduates: how to build something that changes a situation and can still pay for itself.

Role
Teacher
Organisation
National College
Location
Kathmandu

I teach Social Entrepreneurship at National College, Kathmandu, to 32 second-year students across the BDevS, BDFin and BoSS programmes. The course moves from a problem to a venture. How to see a need in your own community clearly rather than through a donor's framing. How to map the people a problem touches. How to design a model that pays for itself, and how to tell whether it has actually changed anything.

We work from Nepali cases, not imported ones. Students spend the term building a venture of their own and defending it to the people who would have to live with it. One group is designing a school for children with disabilities. I have not been able to stop thinking about it since.

I run the course on flipped learning: the reading happens before the room, and the room is for arguing. The hardest part of teaching this cohort is not the content. It is AI. A student can now produce a competent answer to any question I set without having thought about it once, and a venture built that way falls apart the moment someone asks something the model did not anticipate. So I teach them to use it as a tool and not as a substitute, and I keep raising the cost of not having read, not having argued, not having done the work.

What I am really teaching is judgement. Most of my students will not start enterprises. All of them will have to decide, again and again, whether the work in front of them is worth doing, and that is a question of values before it is a question of a business model.
